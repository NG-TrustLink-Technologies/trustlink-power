# TrustLink Power — Hardware Feasibility Study

## 1. Purpose

This document evaluates the technical feasibility of the TrustLink Power concept and defines the boundary between software capabilities and physical hardware requirements.

The objective is to build a realistic engineering foundation before designing or manufacturing dedicated hardware.

---

# 2. Current Problem

Mobile devices depend on battery energy to provide essential services including:

- Communication
- Financial transactions
- Navigation
- Education
- Work activities

Battery limitations can interrupt access to these services.

TrustLink Power investigates ways to improve portable energy availability through a connected software and hardware ecosystem.

---

# 3. Smartphone Battery Transfer Reality

Modern smartphones have strict hardware and software limitations.

A mobile application alone cannot:

- Extract electrical energy from another phone
- Transfer battery power through the internet
- Control another device's battery directly
- Bypass manufacturer charging protections

Battery energy is physical energy and requires a compatible electrical pathway.

---

# 4. Possible Energy Transfer Approaches

## 4.1 Wired Power Transfer

Examples:

- USB-C power delivery
- Charging cables
- Power banks

Advantages:

- Mature technology
- Reliable
- High efficiency

Limitations:

- Requires physical connection
- Requires compatible hardware

---

## 4.2 Wireless Power Transfer

Examples:

- Qi wireless charging
- Magnetic charging systems

Advantages:

- Convenient user experience
- No physical cable

Limitations:

- Requires compatible charging hardware
- Limited distance
- Energy losses occur

---

## 4.3 Dedicated TrustLink Power Hardware

A future TrustLink Power device could provide:

- Battery storage
- Power delivery
- Device communication
- Authentication
- Usage monitoring

Possible components:

- Battery management system
- Microcontroller
- Wireless communication module
- Charging controller
- Security hardware

---

# 5. Software Role

The TrustLink Power software platform can manage:

- User identity
- Device registration
- Power session requests
- Device communication
- Usage history
- Security policies
- Hardware telemetry

The software coordinates the ecosystem but does not create electrical energy transfer by itself.

---

# 6. Hardware Development Requirements

Before manufacturing hardware, engineering validation is required for:

- Battery safety
- Charging standards
- Thermal management
- Power efficiency
- Communication protocols
- Regulatory compliance
- Manufacturing feasibility

---

# 7. MVP Hardware Strategy

The initial MVP will focus on:

- Backend architecture
- Mobile application foundation
- Device management design
- Hardware communication planning

Hardware manufacturing will only begin after technical validation.

---

# 8. Engineering Principles

TrustLink Power hardware development follows:

- Safety before performance
- Standards before custom solutions
- Testing before production
- Reliability before scale
- Evidence-driven decisions
